Stadium Image Calibrator

Fits the exact mapping between the game's ground plane and this stadium's artwork, so measured world coordinates can be drawn on the stylised image with no loss of accuracy. Click the eight landmarks; every one has a known world position already — the infield from the base measurement, the outfield three from the fence measurement.

The outfield three matter most. Fitting on the infield alone spans ~38 units and then extrapolates out to a fence at 80–100, which is where a projective transform is least reliable: tiny clicking error near home becomes tens of feet at the wall, and the wall line drifts off the painted wall even while the diamond looks perfect. Anchoring both foul poles and dead centre makes the fit interpolate across the whole park instead.

The verification is visual. Once fitted, the measured outfield fence is projected through the transform and drawn in green. If it traces the wall in the artwork, the calibration is correct — that curve took no part in the fit.

Two different checks, once the wall pairs are in. The yellow crosses are the height model reproducing your own clicks — they should sit on the pink dots. The pink LINE is something else: it follows the measured fence raised to wall height, not your clicks, so it can run parallel to the wall but offset from it even when the crosses are perfect. That offset means the fence measurement and the painted wall disagree, and the panel reports it in feet.
